    <!-- ========== 1 ========== -->
    <h2 id="viz-profit-map">1. Which of our sales are less profitable? Map of Meaning</h2>
    <p class="viz-link">
      <a href="https://public.tableau.com/views/WhichofoursalesarelessprofitableMapofMeaning/Dashboard1?:language=de-DE&amp;:sid=&amp;:redirect=auth&amp;:display_count=n&amp;:origin=viz_share_link"
         target="_blank" rel="noopener">Open on Tableau Public</a>
    </p>

    <h3>Problem</h3>
    <p>
      Where is the business <strong>losing money</strong>? Leadership needed a geographic
      and product-level view of weak profitability — not only total sales.
    </p>

    <h3>Approach</h3>
    <p>
      Built a <strong>Map of Meaning</strong>–style dashboard combining region and
      sub-category. Used color/size encodings and filters so users can move from
      map overview to the product cuts that drag profit down.
    </p>

    <h3>Result</h3>
    <p>
      A single interactive view that answers: <em>where</em> and <em>in which
      sub-categories</em> sales are less profitable — faster than scanning raw tables.
    </p>

    <!-- ========== 2 ========== -->
    <h2 id="viz-sales-time">2. Total Sales by Category over Time</h2>
    <p class="viz-link">
      <a href="https://public.tableau.com/views/TotalSalesbyCategoryoverTime/TotalSalesbyCategoryoverTime?:language=de-DE&amp;:sid=&amp;:redirect=auth&amp;:display_count=n&amp;:origin=viz_share_link"
         target="_blank" rel="noopener">Open on Tableau Public</a>
    </p>

    <h3>Problem</h3>
    <p>
      Category performance is hard to compare in a static spreadsheet when the
      question is <strong>how sales evolve over time</strong> across categories.
    </p>

    <h3>Approach</h3>
    <p>
      Time-series view of <strong>total sales by category</strong>, with clear series
      separation and interactive focus so trends and crossovers stay readable.
    </p>

    <h3>Result</h3>
    <p>
      A clean sales overview between categories over time — useful for spotting
      growth, seasonality, and relative category strength.
    </p>

    <!-- ========== 3 ========== -->
    <h2 id="viz-superstore">3. Superstore: are we losing or winning?</h2>
    <p class="viz-link">
      <a href="https://public.tableau.com/shared/YZDMBPRMF?:display_count=n&amp;:origin=viz_share_link"
         target="_blank" rel="noopener">Open on Tableau Public</a>
    </p>

    <h3>Problem</h3>
    <p>
      Discounts can lift sales while quietly hurting profit. The business needed a
      view of whether overall performance is <strong>winning or losing</strong> once
      discount and profit are read together — including geography.
    </p>

    <h3>Approach</h3>
    <p>
      Dashboard relating <strong>sales, profit, and discount</strong>, with geographic
      cuts to test whether location explains profit outcomes.
    </p>

    <h3>Result</h3>
    <p>
      Shows the trade-off: discounts can boost sales while profits suffer.
      Reading also suggests <strong>no clear correlation between geography and profits</strong>
      in this Superstore cut — so the lever is more commercial than purely regional.
    </p>

    <!-- ========== 4 ========== -->
    <h2 id="viz-scatter">4. Customer Scatterplot</h2>
    <p class="viz-link">
      <a href="https://public.tableau.com/views/CustomerScatterplot_16934843484120/CustomerScatterplot?:language=de-DE&amp;:sid=&amp;:redirect=auth&amp;:display_count=n&amp;:origin=viz_share_link"
         target="_blank" rel="noopener">Open on Tableau Public</a>
    </p>

    <h3>Problem</h3>
    <p>
      Aggregates hide customer-level patterns. Needed a view of how customers
      spread across key measures (e.g. sales vs profit) to spot outliers and segments.
    </p>

    <h3>Approach</h3>
    <p>
      <strong>Customer-level scatterplot</strong> with interactive selection/tooltips
      so each mark is a customer (or customer grain) rather than a single KPI tile.
    </p>

    <h3>Result</h3>
    <p>
      Quick identification of high-sales/low-profit customers vs healthy ones —
      a starting point for targeting, not only category rollups.

    </p>
